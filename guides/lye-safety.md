# Lye safety

Read this before making any recipe in this repository.

---

## What is lye?

Lye (sodium hydroxide / NaOH for bar soap, potassium hydroxide / KOH for liquid soap) is a caustic alkali required for saponification — the chemical reaction that turns oils into soap.

**Lye is not present in finished soap.** When properly formulated and fully cured, all lye has been consumed in the reaction. However, it is highly dangerous during the soapmaking process.

---

## Required protective equipment

- Safety goggles (not just glasses — splash protection on the sides)
- Nitrile gloves
- Long sleeves
- Closed-toe shoes
- Work in a ventilated area or outdoors

---

## Critical handling rules

1. **Always add lye TO water — never water to lye.** Adding water to lye can cause a violent, spattering exothermic reaction.
2. The lye-water solution will heat to approximately 200°F (93°C) immediately. Do not touch the container.
3. Do not use aluminum containers or utensils. Lye reacts violently with aluminum.
4. Keep children and pets out of the workspace entirely.
5. Label your lye container clearly and store it in a dry, sealed location away from children.

---

## If skin contact occurs

Rinse immediately with large amounts of cool running water for at least 15–20 minutes. Do not use vinegar — it does not neutralize lye on skin and can make things worse. Seek medical attention for significant exposure.

---

## Lye calculators

Always run your recipe through a lye calculator before making it. Do not trust lye amounts from memory or unverified sources.

Recommended calculators:
- [SoapCalc](http://soapcalc.net)
- [Brambleberry Lye Calculator](https://www.brambleberry.com/pages/lye-calculator)
- [Soap Making Friend](https://www.soapmakingfriend.com/soap-making-lye-calculator/)
