# Beginner's guide to cold-process soapmaking

---

## What you'll need

### Equipment
- Digital kitchen scale (measures to 0.1g)
- Two heat-safe containers (stainless steel or HDPE plastic)
- Stick blender
- Thermometer (infrared works well)
- Silicone spatulas
- Soap mold (silicone loaf mold or a lined wooden box)
- Dedicated soap-only equipment — do not reuse for food

### Ingredients
- Base oils (specified per recipe)
- Sodium hydroxide / lye (NaOH)
- Distilled water
- Fragrance or essential oils (optional)
- Colorants, clays, exfoliants (optional)

---

## Key concepts

**Saponification** — The chemical reaction between lye and oils that produces soap and glycerin.

**Superfat (SF%)** — The percentage of oils left unsaponified in the finished bar. A 5% superfat is standard. Higher superfat = richer feel, shorter shelf life.

**Trace** — The point at which the soap batter has emulsified enough to hold a trail when dripped on the surface. Light trace = thin, like pancake batter. Heavy trace = thick, like pudding. Most recipes call for light-to-medium trace before adding fragrance and pouring.

**Cure time** — Soap needs 4–6 weeks to cure after unmolding. During this time, water evaporates and the bar hardens. Do not skip curing — a fully cured bar lasts longer and is gentler.

---

## Basic cold-process steps

1. Weigh all ingredients precisely.
2. In a well-ventilated area, slowly add lye to distilled water. Stir until dissolved. Set aside to cool.
3. Gently melt solid oils; combine with liquid oils. Allow to cool.
4. When both lye-water and oils are between 90–110°F (or within ~10° of each other), slowly pour lye-water into oils.
5. Stick blend to light trace.
6. Add fragrance, colorants, or additives. Blend briefly.
7. Pour into mold. Insulate with a towel for 24–48 hours (helps saponification).
8. Unmold after 24–72 hours. Cut into bars.
9. Cure on a rack with airflow for 4–6 weeks.

---

## Notes on water

Use distilled water. Tap water minerals can affect the reaction unpredictably.

Hard water regions especially should avoid tap water for soapmaking.

## Your first recipe

Start with a simple 3–4 oil recipe with a well-established oil combination (e.g. olive + coconut + castor). Browse the `unscented/` folder for beginner-friendly starting points.
